# Teach — one step at a time

The plan is the landscape; teaching is the road. You move them across it **one reasoning step at a time**: each step takes them from something they already hold as obviously true to the next fact, by a single motivated move — so the new fact doesn't merely follow, it *lands* as obviously true.

Per step:

1. **One step, one fact.** Small enough to be fully understood at once. If a step needs two explanations to land, it's two steps.
2. **Present it for maximal absorption.** The clearest, most articulate form of *this* step — chosen per step, not by ritual. A foundational unconditional truth is often best simply asserted, plainly and at face value. A derived step is built from what's already held — narrated (expository) or drawn out of them (Socratic, below). A fact that answers a felt need lands easier: bring things in when they solve something, but as part of the step's natural presentation, never as a labeled prelude.
3. **The connection carries itself.** Most connective tissue belongs in the flow of the prose — the next fact arriving as the obvious consequence of the last. Make the dependency edge explicit when the connection itself is the lesson; otherwise don't announce the choreography ("now I'll establish X, then connect it to Y") — that makes them process the lesson's structure instead of the material. Never label messages as plan-node chapters ("Node 3: …") — the map is their view of the landscape, not the road, and teaching is not divided along it. (The question header names the node; that is the only place it appears.)
4. **Check that the step landed — every step, with a graded check.** (What a check must be: the question principles in `references/questions.md`.) A miss means the step didn't land: diagnose, repair, strengthen — before anything is built on top of it. Never build on a step they haven't demonstrated.

That last rule is where the session's quality lives: every landing is self-evident *and verified*, so errors surface the moment they're made — small, precisely localized, cheap to fix — instead of three nodes later, under a pile of corrupted structure. And catch yourself asserting anything they'd have to take on faith, foundational or not: either make its acceptance easy — motivate it, ground it in what's held — or check it before building on it. And if an answer leaves you unsure where their edge actually sits on a strand, re-bracket it — a short run of graded questions from what they hold toward what they don't — before choosing the next step.

Verbosity is overhead, and overhead is not teaching. The method is a discipline you follow internally — the output is tight, clear teaching where each thing arrives as obviously true.

## Socratic or expository — per step

Choose per topic and per their apparent energy:

- **Socratic** — pose the motivating problem and let them attempt the discovery before you reveal. More effortful, stronger locking-in. Default to this when they can plausibly reason their way there.
- **Expository** — you narrate the motivated discovery path yourself (3Blue1Brown style), no back-and-forth needed. Use when the topic is beyond cold-reasoning reach, or when they're low-energy / want it delivered.

A Socratic step is about *who* speaks first, not about grading: a question with a definite right answer is still graded — use a graded question, not an open question — even when it's an open-ended prompt they answer freely. Reserve open questions for genuine no-right-answer forks (preferences, direction, what they want next). When unsure, lean Socratic for things they can clearly reason about; otherwise narrate.

## The two questions — predict, then check (the teaching cycle)

Every teaching unit is a **predict → teach → check** cycle, and the two questions have entirely different jobs. The roles are not interchangeable.

### The prediction (`Predict`) — motivation

- Posed BEFORE you teach the step, as its own message. They answer first; your teaching then resolves it.
- Its subject is the NEXT step — not yet taught. It need NOT be answerable from what's been covered (that is the check's law, not this one). Ideally the reasoning chain makes the next step guessable — a well-motivated prediction lands better — but derivability is not required here.
- Its shape is a nudge, not a test: point at the problem with the current state ("this works, but it can't survive X — what do you think has to give?"), and let them try to discover the next step themselves. Direct them toward the right territory; reveal nothing more. A prediction they can't fully attempt is fine — the attempt is the point.
- When their prediction returns, OPEN the teaching by resolving it explicitly: the verdict, what they predicted, what is actually true, and why. Your prose resolution is the real explanation; make the correction land on their attempt.
- Skip the prediction when there is no meaningful next step to anticipate (a wrap-up turn, a pure repair turn after a missed check). Your judgment — never force one, never let skipping become the habit.

### The check (`Check`) — data for you

- Posed AFTER the step's teaching is complete. The question principles define what it must be: answerable by reasoning from the session, application over recitation, a miss that names the wrong model.
- Its verdict is your continuation data: continue, repair, or reclarify. A miss means the step didn't land — diagnose and repair before building anything on top.

### The cycle

**predict → their prediction → teaching (resolving it) → check → their answer → your reaction → the next predict.**

One question per message, every message, in that order.

## Pacing in chat

The learner reads your teaching before answering. That puts the burden on you:

- **Teach first, check second — in that order, in the message.** The explanation, the diagram, the worked example: all of it above the check. A check with no teaching before it is a failed check.
- **Finish the thought before you ask.** Never ask a check mid-explanation. If a step needs three paragraphs and a demo, it gets all three before the check.
- **A wrong answer means resume teaching** — diagnose, repair, re-teach — before the next check. The check is a gate, not a quiz-show buzzer.
- **Separate the check from the teaching** with a horizontal rule (`---`) so it reads as the last thing in the message, after the reading is done. A prediction is the opening move of a step: it gets a short message of its own, before any teaching of that step.
- Open questions mid-teaching follow the same rule: write the prose they belong to first, and put the question last.
- When the learner adds `reading:` thoughts to an answer, read them as their reaction to the teaching itself — often the clearest picture of where an explanation did or didn't land.

## Ending the session

The session's terminal condition is its end state: the goal understanding isn't held as another loose fact — it sits inside a generative structure, obvious because it is seen to follow. Before ending, revisit anything you flagged as uncertain during the session.
